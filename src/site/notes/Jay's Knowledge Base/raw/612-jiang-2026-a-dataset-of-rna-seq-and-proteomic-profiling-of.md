## Abstract

Inflammatory bowel disease involves chronic inflammation of the intestine and disruption of normal immune balance, often linked to gut microbiota dysbiosis. The beneficial bacterium Akkermansia muciniphila (A.muciniphila) is known to support intestinal health and immune regulation, but its specific effects on T cell responses remain unclear. Here, we present a comprehensive multi-omics dataset integrating RNA sequencing and quantitative proteomics analyses of splenic CD4⁺ T cells derived from wild-type C57BL/6 J mice subjected to dextran sulfate sodium elicited colitis, in the presence or absence of A.muciniphila supplementation. The dataset includes transcriptomic profiles, protein abundance quantification, and quality control metrics for reproducibility. These data provide a valuable resource for exploring how A.muciniphila modulates immune metabolism, signaling, and inflammatory pathways in CD4⁺ T cells during intestinal inflammation, supporting future multi-omics integration and comparative analyses of host–microbe interactions in IBD models.
