## Abstract

Sarcopenia, characterized by the progressive loss of skeletal muscle mass, strength, and function, represents a major public health challenge affecting more than 50 million older adults worldwide. Its multifactorial aetiology includes diminished satellite cell activity, mitochondrial dysfunction, chronic low-grade inflammation, hormonal alterations, and changes in myocellular signalling, ultimately impairing mobility, metabolic health, and quality of life. Recent evidence highlights the gut microbiome (GM) as a key mediator of muscle physiology through its regulation of energy metabolism, immune balance, neuromuscular integrity, and the availability of bioactive gut-derived metabolites such as short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs), amino acids, and vitamins that favourably modulate its functionality. In contrast, dysbiosis, the imbalance in GM composition and function, adversely alters these pathways, promoting chronic inflammation, impaired anabolism, and functional decline that affect skeletal muscle health. Physical exercise is considered the most effective non-pharmacological strategy for preventing and treating sarcopenia, largely through its ability to activate anabolic signalling pathways, enhance mitochondrial biogenesis, and preserve neuromuscular function. Emerging evidence shows that exercise also alters GM composition, increasing taxa associated with metabolic efficiency, enhanced barrier function, reduced inflammation, and SCFA production through the gut–muscle axis. Microbiome-derived metabolites support protein synthesis, satellite cell renewal, and oxidative muscle metabolism, suggesting that this bi-directional gut–muscle axis is modulated by exercise training. This narrative review synthesizes current evidence on the cellular and molecular mechanisms underlying sarcopenia and evaluates how exercise-induced modulation of the GM may protect against muscle atrophy. We explore key mechanistic evidence demonstrating how targeted GM interventions and structured exercise regimens may synergistically counteract muscle decline. Understanding these interconnected pathways may support the development of innovative therapeutic approaches combining exercise and GM modulation to improve muscle health, functional independence, and healthy ageing trajectories.
