# Microencapsulation strategy for aerobic cultivation of anaerobic gut bacteria for CRC intervention

## Abstract

Akkermansia muciniphila is a next generation probiotic (NGP) that is associated with colorectal cancer prevention. However, it is limited by its high sensitivity to oxygen exposure, which significantly reduces its viability during processing and storage. This project aims to evaluate the factors affecting the survivability of A. muciniphila and to enhance its survivability through encapsulation using alginate based microcapsules. Experimental studies were conducted to assess bacterial viability under different environmental conditions, including aerobic and anaerobic incubation, and time-dependent survivability in aerobic conditions over time. In addition, sodium citrate at varying concentrations was used to dissolve alginate beads to determine its effect on the viable cells. Encapsulation of A. muciniphila was performed using sodium alginate and resistant starch via extrusion, followed by evaluation of bacterial growth within microcapsules and stability during storage. Results showed that A. muciniphila exhibited significantly higher viability under anaerobic conditions, while prolonged exposure to aerobic conditions resulted in viability falling below the limit of detection. In a non-nutritive environment, higher temperatures accelerated cell death, whereas lower temperatures better preserved bacterial viability over time. The concentration of sodium citrate did not significantly affect bacterial viability, indicating that effective bead dissolution can be achieved without compromising cell survival. Furthermore, encapsulated A. muciniphila demonstrated improved stability and maintained higher viability during storage compared to free cells. In conclusion, environmental conditions play a critical role in determining the survival of A. muciniphila, and encapsulation using alginate-based systems is an effective strategy to enhance its stability. These findings support the development of stable delivery systems for oxygen-sensitive probiotics with potential applications in colorectal cancer intervention.
