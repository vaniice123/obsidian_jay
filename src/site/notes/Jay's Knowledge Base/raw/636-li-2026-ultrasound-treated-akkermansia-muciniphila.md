# Ultrasound-treated *Akkermansia muciniphila* postbiotics loaded with butyrylated starch-coated colon-targeted pellets: Time and dose dependent modulation of human gut microbiota

## Abstract

Akkermansia muciniphila, a promising next-generation probiotic, faces severe bottlenecks in functional food applications due to its stringent growth requirements. Here, A. muciniphila postbiotics were prepared via mucin supplementation combined with ultrasonic-assisted heat inactivation (U300W), and characterized by untargeted metabolomics. Compared with conventionally heat-inactivated postbiotics (FM0%), U300W exhibited a distinct metabolite composition, enriched in amino acids (arginine, leucine, histidyl–proline), purine metabolites (hypoxanthine), and organic acid derivatives. Based on this metabolomic superiority, U300W postbiotics were subsequently incorporated into colon-targeted pellets with gradient loading (A0–A100) using extrusion–spheronization and butyrylated starch coating. In vitro fecal fermentation revealed that the postbiotics-loaded pellets exerted time- and dose-dependent regulatory effects on the gut microbiota. High-loading pellets (A80, A100) significantly enhanced microbial α-diversity, reduced the Firmicutes/Bacteroidota (F/B) ratio, suppressed opportunistic pathogens, and enriched Bifidobacterium and Bacteroides. A fermentation-phase-dependent substrate shift was observed: carbohydrate-metabolizing taxa (e.g., Bifidobacterium) predominated at 24 h, whereas amino acid-metabolizing syntrophic bacteria (e.g., Anaerotignum, Synergistaceae) were selectively enriched at 48 h PICRUSt2 analysis further computationally predicted dose-dependent upregulation of amino acid metabolism, carbohydrate metabolism, and glycan biosynthesis pathways. Overall, these results indicate the microbiota-modulating potential of metabolically optimized A. muciniphila postbiotics delivered via colon-targeted pellets in an in vitro fermentation model, with functional interpretations based on computational prediction.
