## Abstract

As demand for GLP-1 based weight loss drugs explodes worldwide, and patents expire in some countries, generic manufacturers are stepping up production to gain a slice of the market. We look at what’s coming to the market where, and what the effect might be on price and availability A major drawback of glucagon-like peptide 1 (GLP-1) weight loss drugs is their cost. So, as early patents begin to expire, generic versions, which should be significantly cheaper than the patented drugs, are entering the market. Barbara McGowan, professor of endocrinology and diabetes at King’s College London, and chief medical officer at Reset Health, told The BMJ that this could have implications for their access in the health service. “In the NHS, generics may increase access, which is currently very restricted,” she said. She explained that access to GLP-1s is already being rationed on the health service.1 This rationing has widened inequalities when it comes to accessing these drugs, as those without an NHS prescription have to pay privately. But a new entry of generic GLP-1s for weight loss could change that.
