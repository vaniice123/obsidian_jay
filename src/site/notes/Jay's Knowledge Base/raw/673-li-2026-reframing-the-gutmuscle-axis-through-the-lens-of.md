## Abstract

Sarcopenia is a progressive systemic skeletal muscle disorder that predominantly affects older adults, and its pathogenesis remains incompletely understood. Research on sarcopenia has largely followed two lines of inquiry. The first concerns chronic, age-associated sarcopenia, which involves anabolic resistance, chronic low-grade inflammation, neuromuscular junction degeneration, and hormonal alterations. The second concerns acute sarcopenia and related rapid muscle wasting during severe illness (e.g. sepsis, trauma, major surgery), which must be distinguished from ICU-acquired weakness and cancer cachexia. The gut–muscle axis has attracted interest as a potential contributor to sarcopenia onset and progression. This narrative review integrates heterogeneous cell, animal, observational human, and intervention evidence through four proposed functional modules: anabolic support, anti-catabolic protection, redox/mitochondrial homeostasis, and inflammatory–immune regulation. From the perspective of functional redundancy, we discuss a hypothetical “sarcopenia-susceptible state” and stage-informed research questions; these are conceptual constructs rather than validated disease stages or treatment algorithms. Microbiota-related interventions are candidate adjuncts to nutrition and exercise. Purified metabolites are distinguished from postbiotics, and direct clinical efficacy in acute critical-illness sarcopenia remains unestablished.
