## Abstract

**Background**: The rapid adoption of glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1 RAs) and dual incretin therapies, particularly semaglutide and tirzepatide, represents a transformative development in obesity management with implications extending beyond clinical practice. Originally developed for type 2 diabetes, these therapies induce substantial and sustained weight loss by suppressing appetite, enhancing satiety, and altering food preferences, with the potential to reshape population-level food consumption. Despite extensive evidence of their clinical efficacy, their broader implications for food systems remain largely unexplored.

**Scope**: AND APPROACH: This narrative review synthesises peer-reviewed clinical, nutritional, consumer-behaviour, and food-system evidence alongside industry and market intelligence. To address the limitations of the current data landscape, evidence was classified into three distinct tiers established, emerging, or scenario-based according to the methodology and empirical strength of the supporting data. Drawing on literature from nutrition, consumer behaviour, agricultural economics, and food systems science, the review evaluates potential impacts across agricultural production, food manufacturing, retail, food service, and global supply chains.

**Key Findings and Conclusions**: Increasing GLP-1 adoption may contribute to changes in food purchasing, product reformulation, marketing and retail strategies, and demand for nutrient-dense and protein-rich foods. However, empirical evidence for population-level food-system effects remains limited and heterogeneous. Much of the available data is derived from short-term clinical trials, localized observational analyses, household purchasing data, and commercial industry reports rather than long-term, population-scale systems studies. The review identifies key knowledge gaps, including the critical need to quantify long-term dietary transitions, treatment persistence, and their subsequent consequences for global food production, resource use, and environmental sustainability. By integrating evidence across health, nutrition, and food systems disciplines, this review provides an interdisciplinary framework for examining how sustained GLP-1 use could hypothetically influence food demand and the wider food economy, while highlighting priorities for future empirical research and policy preparedness.
