## Abstract

Intestinal glucagon‐like peptide‐1 (GLP‐1) is secreted by intestinal L cells in a glucose‐dependent manner. The localization and surface abundance of glucose‐sensing proteins on the cell membrane affect the response to glucose, but the regulatory mechanism remains poorly understood. Here, we show that S‐palmitoylation is required for stable plasma membrane enrichment of SGLT1 and for SGLT1‐mediated GLP‐1 secretion. Notably, myostatin (MSTN) was identified as a key upstream regulator of this modification. Intestinal MSTN deficiency markedly enhanced SGLT1 palmitoylation, increased its plasma membrane abundance, and thereby promoted glucose‐stimulated GLP‐1 secretion. Importantly, simvastatin induced intestinal MSTN upregulation and impaired glucose homeostasis, whereas MSTN deficiency largely alleviated these effects. Mechanistically, MSTN regulated the expression of the palmitoyl acyltransferase ZDHHC20, which catalyzed SGLT1 S‐palmitoylation at Cys301. Blocking SGLT1 palmitoylation attenuated SGLT1 membrane enrichment and markedly suppressed the induction of GLP‐1 secretion caused by MSTN deficiency. Collectively, our research results have revealed a new mechanism by which MSTN regulates intestinal endocrine function and S‐palmitoylation modification promotes GLP‐1 secretion, providing potential targets for regulating endogenous GLP‐1 secretion in the intestine and treating metabolic diseases related to glucose imbalance., Intestinal MSTN deficiency enhances glucose‐stimulated GLP‐1 secretion and glucose tolerance in high‐fat diet‐fed mice. Mechanistically, loss of MSTN promotes YY1‐associated ZDHHC20 expression, increasing SGLT1 S‐palmitoylation at Cys301 and its plasma membrane localization in L cells. Additionally, simvastatin increases intestinal MSTN expression, impairing GLP‐1 secretion and glucose homeostasis.
