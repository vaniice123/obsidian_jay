# Efficacy and Safety of Diclofenac–Hyaluronate Conjugate (Diclofenac Etalhyaluronate) for Knee Osteoarthritis: A Randomized Phase III Trial in Japan

## Abstract

Objective To confirm the efficacy and safety of intra-articular injection of diclofenac covalently linked to hyaluronic acid (diclofenac etalhyaluronate [DF-HA]: ONO-5704/SI-613). Methods In a multicenter, randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind phase 3 trial, eligible patients aged 40–75 years with symptomatic knee osteoarthritis (OA; Kellgren–Lawrence score of 2 or 3) were randomly assigned to DF-HA 30 mg or placebo (citric acid-sodium citrate buffered solution; 1:1), which was injected into the knee joint cavity six times, once every 4 weeks for 20 weeks. The follow-up period was 24 weeks. The primary endpoint was 12-week mean change from baseline in Western Ontario and McMaster University Osteoarthritis 3.1 Index (WOMAC) pain subscores on a 100-mm visual analog scale. Safety was evaluated by adverse event monitoring. Results All 440 subjects (placebo: 220; DF-HA: 220) received investigational products. Full analysis set and safety population comprised 438 (placebo: 220; DF-HA: 218) and 440 subjects. The primary endpoint was a statistically significant improvement over placebo (placebo: −17.1 mm, DF-HA: −23.2 mm; difference: −6.1 mm, 95% confidence interval: −9.4 to −2.8; p<0.001). The effect was statistically significant between groups as early as 1 week, and a difference was maintained for 24 weeks, although no statistically significant difference was observed at week 24. Anaphylactic reactions were observed in two subjects receiving DF-HA. Conclusion We achieved the primary objective of a statistically significant improvement in WOMAC pain subscores over placebo during 12 weeks of DF-HA. Anaphylactic reactions were observed, and further safety evaluation is needed.
