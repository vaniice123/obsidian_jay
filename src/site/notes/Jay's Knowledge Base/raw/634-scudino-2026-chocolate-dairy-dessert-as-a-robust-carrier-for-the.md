# Chocolate dairy dessert as a robust carrier for the next-generation probiotic *Akkermansia muciniphila*: gastrointestinal survival and biological activity

## Abstract

The incorporation of next-generation probiotics (NGP) into food systems remains constrained by technological and stability challenges. This study evaluated the feasibility of using a chocolate-flavored dairy dessert as a carrier for Akkermansia muciniphila, focusing on NGP viability, survival under simulated gastrointestinal conditions, microbiological stability, biological activity, amino acid profile, and sensory acceptance during 28 days of aerobic refrigerated storage (4 ± 2 °C). Desserts were formulated with 0, 8, 10, and 12 log CFU/g of A. muciniphila to assess dose-dependent behavior. High viability throughout storage was observed, with counts consistently above levels commonly recommended for probiotic products. Under simulated gastrointestinal conditions, reductions ranged from 0.9 to 1.5 log CFU/g, indicating that the dairy dessert provided protective effects during in vitro digestion. Probiotic incorporation was also associated with lower levels of background microbiota. Finally, the addition of A. muciniphila generated a favorable amino acid profile and good sensory acceptance, as well as increased proteolytic activity and enhanced in vitro antioxidant, antihypertensive, antidiabetic, and anti-obesogenic activities in a concentration-dependent manner. Overall, incorporating A. muciniphila into a chocolate dairy dessert under realistic aerobic storage conditions supported its stability and biological activity, with potential for application in innovative NGP food systems.
