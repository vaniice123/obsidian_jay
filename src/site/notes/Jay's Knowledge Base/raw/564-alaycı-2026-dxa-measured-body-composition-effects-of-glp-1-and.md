# DXA-Measured Body Composition Effects of GLP-1 and Dual GIP/GLP-1 Receptor Agonists in Adults with Obesity or Type 2 Diabetes: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Phase 3 Randomized Trials

## Abstract

**Background**: Glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) and dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonists produce substantial weight loss in adults with obesity and type 2 diabetes (T2D), but it comprises both fat and lean tissue, and the absolute magnitude of lean body mass (LBM) loss on dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA) — the gold-standard modality — remains uncertain. We quantified DXA-measured LBM and fat mass change, in absolute kilograms, and characterised its variability across populations.

**Methods**: We performed a systematic review and meta-analysis of phase 3 randomized controlled trials comparing GLP-1 or dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonists with placebo or an active comparator for at least 24 weeks and reporting DXA-derived LBM and fat mass in adults with obesity or T2D. PubMed, Scopus, and Cochrane CENTRAL were searched from inception to 23 March 2026 (ClinicalTrials.gov for registration verification). Pooled mean differences (MD) were estimated using random-effects restricted maximum likelihood with Hartung–Knapp–Sidik–Jonkman (HKSJ) variance correction, with frequentist and Bayesian sensitivity analyses and pre-specified subgroup analysis by population. Risk of bias was assessed with the Cochrane Risk of Bias 2.0 tool and certainty of evidence with GRADE.

**Results**: Five phase 3 trials (505 DXA-evaluated adults) were included. LBM was reduced (MD − 1.96 kg; 95% CI − 3.64 to − 0.27; I² = 87.8%), as was fat mass (MD − 5.29 kg; 95% CI − 10.78 to + 0.21; I² = 94.6%), with fat reduction predominating (≈ 2.5–3 kg fat per kg lean); alternative and Bayesian models all returned clearly negative fat-mass estimates. In the T2D subgroup (k = 3), the LBM effect was homogeneous and consistent (MD − 1.21 kg; 95% CI − 2.33 to − 0.09; I² = 5.3%; GRADE moderate). In obesity (k = 2), both changes were larger but imprecise. Overall certainty of evidence was low for LBM and very low for fat mass.

**Conclusions**: Incretin therapy reduces both LBM and fat mass on DXA, with fat loss predominating; the LBM reduction is small (approximately 1 to 2 kg) and, in T2D, consistent and clinically anticipatable. Baseline body composition assessment, adequate dietary protein, and concurrent resistance training are advisable, particularly where sarcopenia risk is present.
