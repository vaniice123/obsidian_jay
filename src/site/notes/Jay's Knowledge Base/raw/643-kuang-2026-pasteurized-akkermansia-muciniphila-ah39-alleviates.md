# Pasteurized *Akkermansia muciniphila* AH39 alleviates high-fat diet-induced obesity by modulating the gut microbiota-bile acid metabolism-FXR pathway

## Abstract

Obesity is commonly associated with chronic low-grade inflammation and gut microbial dysbiosis. Akkermansia muciniphila has emerged as a promising next-generation beneficial bacterium for the amelioration of metabolic dysregulation. To evaluate the anti-obesity effects of pasteurized A. muciniphila AH39 (CCFM1083), 18 male C57BL/6J mice were randomly assigned to three groups (CON, HFD, and AH39; n = 6 per group) and underwent a 12-week intervention. These results showed that pasteurized AH39 markedly attenuated HFD-induced body weight gain. AH39 intervention was also accompanied by reductions in hepatic steatosis, improvements in glucose tolerance, insulin sensitivity, and serum lipid and liver injury markers, and attenuation of systemic inflammation and hepatic oxidative stress. Gut microbiome profiling further showed that AH39 intervention was associated with remodeling of the microbial community, characterized by increased relative abundances of Parabacteroides, Roseburia, and Muribaculum and decreased relative abundances of Alistipes and Tyzzerella. Targeted metabolomic analyses further revealed alterations in the hepatic bile acid profile, characterized by increases in CA, CDCA, β-MCA, T-β-MCA, TCA, and TCDCA and decreases in DCA and TLCA. At the molecular level, AH39 intervention coincided with altered intestinal transcript levels of FXR–FGF15-related genes. Collectively, these findings indicate that pasteurized A. muciniphila AH39 attenuates HFD-induced obesity and associated metabolic dysfunction, in association with coordinated remodeling of the gut microbiota, bile acid metabolism, and FXR-FGF15-related transcriptional responses in the gut-liver axis. These findings provide a theoretical basis for the potential application of pasteurized AH39 as a postbiotic intervention for metabolic disorders.
