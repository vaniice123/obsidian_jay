## Abstract

The gut microbiome, the community of bacteria residing the lower gastrointestinal tract, is increasingly recognized as an important influence on skeletal muscle physiology, with critical implications for muscle disorders such as sarcopenia and muscular dystrophy. Researchers have uncovered multiple microbiome-derived metabolites that alter skeletal muscle protein synthesis, mitochondrial biogenesis, protein degradation, and glucose transport. Moreover, the microbiome mediates systemic cytokine release of interleukin-6 and tumor necrosis factor alpha, which can drive muscle loss. These studies have led to the identification of gut bacterial species (such as Veillonella atypica and Roseburia inulinivorans) that improve muscle endurance and strength when administered as probiotics in animal models. Several clinical trials of microbiome-focused therapies have also been conducted, with suggestion of benefits as well as potential harms. Taken together, the gut microbiome plays a key role in modulating skeletal muscle physiology, and may serve as the basis for novel therapeutics targeting muscle disease.
