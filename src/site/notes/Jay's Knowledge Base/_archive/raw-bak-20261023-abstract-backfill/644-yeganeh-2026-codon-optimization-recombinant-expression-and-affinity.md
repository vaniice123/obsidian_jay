## Abstract

The P9 outer membrane protein of Akkermansia muciniphila has emerged as a promising therapeutic candidate for metabolic disorders, including type 2 diabetes. However, obtaining high-yield, soluble recombinant P9 for downstream functional studies is often restricted by codon usage bias and folding limitations in heterologous hosts. This study aimed to design, codon-optimize, express, and purify recombinant P9 protein in Escherichia coli BL21 (DE3) using the pET-28a(+) expression system. The P9 coding sequence was codon-optimized, synthesized, and cloned into the pET-28a(+) vector. Following transformation into E. coli BL21 (DE3), protein expression was induced with 0.5 mM isopropyl β-D-1-thiogalactopyranoside (IPTG). Cellular solubility was determined by SDS-PAGE analysis of soluble and insoluble lysate fractions. Purification efficiency was evaluated and compared using different nickel-affinity chromatography configurations, including fast protein liquid chromatography (FPLC) and gravity-flow columns. The purity of the purified protein was assessed by SDS-PAGE, and its concentration was quantified using the Bradford assay; protein identity was supported by agreement with the predicted molecular weight rather than by immunological verification, as anti-His Western blot confirmation was not performed in this study. Codon optimization yielded a Codon Adaptation Index (CAI) of 0.81 and adjusted the GC content to a uniform ~51%, with qualitative replacement of rare codons and a more uniform GC distribution; the exact magnitude of improvement relative to the native sequence could not be calculated, as numerical data for the native sequence were not available in the original optimization report. SDS-PAGE analysis revealed that recombinant P9 was expressed predominantly in the soluble fraction. The optimized purification strategy using nickel-agarose chromatography coupled with FPLC-compatible buffers yielded a highly purified, single protein band at approximately 83 kDa, matching the theoretical molecular weight. The identity of the target His-tagged P9 protein was consistent with its predicted molecular weight, although anti-His Western blot confirmation was not performed in this study; the final concentration of purified recombinant P9 was determined to be 0.235±0.018 µg/µL (mean SD). It has been established an efficient, reproducible, and optimized prokaryotic expression and purification platform for producing soluble, high-purity recombinant P9 protein, providing a solid methodological basis for subsequent in vivo biomedical applications.
